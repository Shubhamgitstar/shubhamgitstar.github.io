How I Find What I Find

Not exploits, not zero-days — just knowing where to look, and enough scripting discipline to look at scale instead of one URL at a time.

Search-Engine Dorking

Advanced Google/Shodan/Censys dork strings to surface exposed config files, panels, and directories across large index sets — the same footprint an attacker would map first.

Cyber Threat Intelligence

Monitoring leak forums and stealer-log marketplaces for credentials tied to Indian domains, correlating exposure back to a real, ownable incident rather than raw noise.

Bash & Recon Scripting

Lightweight shell tooling to batch-verify dork hits, de-duplicate false positives, and triage severity before a single report gets written.

Coordinated Disclosure

PROTOCOL

How a Finding Becomes a Report

01 — CONFIRM

Verify the exposure is real. Nothing further is downloaded, logged into, or extracted beyond what proves the issue.

02 — REDACT

Every credential, key, and identifier is fully masked before it leaves my machine — partial redaction isn't redaction.

03 — IDENTIFY

The affected organization is confirmed through public registry data, not guessed, before a report is filed.

04 — REPORT

Filed to CERT-In and, where practical, directly to the affected organization in parallel.

05 — WAIT

A reasonable remediation window is given before any public reference to the finding — sector and category only, never identifying detail.

Reports that make systems safer, not headlines.
